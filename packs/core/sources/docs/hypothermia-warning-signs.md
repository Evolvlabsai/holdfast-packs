# Hypothermia warning signs

Source: CDC — Hypothermia and cold stress (public domain)

Hypothermia happens when the body loses heat faster than it can make it, and it can set in at
temperatures well above freezing — especially when someone is wet, tired, or has been still for a
long time. Call 911 for anyone you think has hypothermia.

## Warning signs in adults and older children

- Shivering — and, later, shivering that stops even though the person is still cold
- Slurred or mumbled speech
- Clumsy hands and stumbling
- Confusion, poor judgement, drowsiness
- Slow, shallow breathing and a weak pulse

## Warning signs in babies

- Bright red, cold skin
- Very low energy and an unusually quiet, weak cry

## What to do while help is coming

1. Get the person into a warm room or shelter and handle them gently.
2. Remove any wet clothing and dry the skin.
3. Warm the centre of the body first — chest, neck, head and groin — with blankets, dry clothing or
   skin-to-skin contact under a blanket. Do not rub or massage the arms and legs.
4. Give warm, sweet, non-alcoholic drinks only if the person is fully awake and able to swallow.
   Never give alcohol.
5. Once they begin to warm, keep the whole body including the head covered, and keep them still.

Do not use a hot bath, a heating pad or a heat lamp: warming the skin too quickly can cause a
dangerous drop in blood pressure or an irregular heartbeat. Someone with severe hypothermia may
appear dead — keep warming them and keep them still until medical help takes over.
