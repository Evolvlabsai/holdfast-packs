# Disinfecting water with household bleach

Source: CDC — Making Water Safe in an Emergency; EPA — Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water (public domain)

Use unscented liquid household chlorine bleach only when you cannot boil. Bleach kills most germs
but is less reliable than boiling, and it does nothing about chemical contamination.

## Check the bottle first

Use plain, unscented household bleach whose only active ingredient is sodium hypochlorite. Do not
use scented bleach, colour-safe bleach, bleach with added cleaners, or pool chlorine. Bleach loses
strength on the shelf: replace the bottle you keep for water treatment every year.

## How much to add, per gallon of water

| Bleach strength | Clear water | Cloudy water (filtered and settled first) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6% sodium hypochlorite | 8 drops | 16 drops |
| 8.25% sodium hypochlorite | 6 drops | 12 drops |

## Steps

1. Filter and settle cloudy water first.
2. Add the drops, put the lid on, and shake or stir well.
3. Wait 30 minutes.
4. The water should have a slight chlorine smell. If it does not, repeat the dose and wait another
   15 minutes before using it.

If the water still has no chlorine smell after a second dose, discard it and find another source.
Keep the treated water in clean, covered containers out of sunlight.

## Water purification tablets

Commercial water purification tablets (chlorine dioxide or iodine) are an alternative when you
cannot boil and have no bleach. Follow the package directions exactly, including the waiting time,
which is longer for cold or cloudy water. Iodine tablets are not for pregnant women, people with
thyroid problems or long-term use.
