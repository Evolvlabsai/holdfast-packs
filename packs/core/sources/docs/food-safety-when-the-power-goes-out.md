# Food safety when the power goes out

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service — Food Safety During a Power Outage (public domain)

Cold food stays safe for a limited and predictable time. Eat it in that order and almost nothing is
wasted; open the doors to check and you lose hours of safe storage.

## How long cold food lasts

- An unopened refrigerator keeps food safe for about 4 hours.
- A full, unopened freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours.
- A half-full freezer holds for about 24 hours.

Keep both doors shut. Group frozen packages together so they help keep each other cold, and move
anything you will need soon to the front before the power goes out if you have warning.

## Eat-first order

1. Refrigerated food — milk, leftovers, cooked meat, soft cheese and anything already opened.
2. Freezer food, starting with whatever has begun to soften.
3. Pantry food, earliest expiry date first.
4. Long-shelf food — canned goods, rice, pasta and long-life rations — last.

## When the power comes back

Throw out any perishable food — meat, poultry, fish, eggs, leftovers — that has been above 40 °F
(4 °C) for 2 hours or more. Food in the freezer can be refrozen if it still has ice crystals or is
at 40 °F or below. When in doubt, throw it out: you cannot tell by smell or by looking whether food
is safe.

An appliance thermometer in the fridge and one in the freezer turn all of this from guesswork into a
reading you can trust.
