# Making water safe by boiling

Source: CDC — Making Water Safe in an Emergency (public domain)

Boiling is the surest way to kill bacteria, viruses and parasites in water. Use it whenever you have
heat and fuel, and treat boiling as the first choice over chemical disinfection.

## Before you boil

If the water is cloudy, let it settle so the sediment falls to the bottom, then pour the clear water
through a clean cloth, a paper towel or a coffee filter. Filtering does not make water safe on its
own, but it removes the particles that protect germs from heat and from disinfectant.

## Boiling

1. Bring the water to a rolling boil — a boil you can see rolling over on itself, not just bubbles
   forming on the bottom of the pan.
2. Hold the rolling boil for 1 minute.
3. At elevations above 6,500 feet (about 2,000 metres), hold the rolling boil for 3 minutes instead.
4. Let the water cool on its own. Do not add ice or cold tap water to speed it up.

## Afterwards

Store the cooled water in clean, covered containers and keep them out of direct sunlight. Boiled
water tastes flat because the dissolved air has been driven off; pouring it between two clean
containers, or adding a pinch of salt per quart, improves the taste.

Boiling does not remove chemical contamination, fuel or heavy metals. If your water may be
chemically contaminated, do not drink it — use bottled water or water from another source.
